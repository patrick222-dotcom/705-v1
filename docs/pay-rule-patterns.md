# Pay-rule patterns across police, nursing and other shift work: what the pay model would need

Research date 2026-10-10. **Research only** — nothing here changes `index.html`, and every code gap
below that moves a dollar figure is Invariant 3 work. Successor to `police-pay-rules-draft.md`, which
asked the question for one township; this asks it for the job: **what would the pay model need so a
police officer, a nurse, or another hourly worker with an unusual pay structure gets a figure within a
few percent — and does that require any one employer's contract?** Every rule below was read in the
contract text unless marked **unverified**.

**The sample is chosen for variety, not drawn at random**: 21 police contracts from 9 states, 4 nurse
contracts, 5 other hourly contracts. Counts like "13 of 21" describe this sample, not the country.

## Verdict

1. **No Right-to-Know request is needed.** Patterns are fully reachable from public sources: Ohio
   (766 current police bargaining units, free PDFs), New Jersey (555 police-union contracts running to
   2024 or later, plus 556 interest-arbitration awards) and Iowa all publish current contracts by
   statute, and big cities post their own. Pennsylvania publishes nothing centrally — Act 111 awards are
   sent only "to both parties to the dispute" — but the app never needs a contract: **the officer
   brings their own numbers, from a stub.** An RTK request is worth it only to build a preset for one
   department that becomes a real user cohort, and even then a stub is faster and more accurate.
2. **8-hour police fit today with configuration. Most other schedules do not.** Each schedule needs a
   different overtime setting, and **12-hour tours — 6 of 21 contracts — have no correct setting at
   all**: the app adds overtime the contract doesn't pay, +4.8% to +16.7% of gross every fortnight
   (probed below).
3. **Five primitives close most of the gap**, in this order: a configurable work period
   (including "overtime only where I flag it"); a per-shift multiplier plus minimum-hours events;
   dated lump-sum pay items; salaried base pay; and differential designation (start / majority /
   clock window). Every one is wage-core. Section (d) ranks all twelve found.

**What the pattern says.** Police pay is a salary-or-hourly base, plus overtime triggered by leaving the
schedule rather than by crossing 40, plus a long tail of *events* (court, call-back, holiday worked,
details) with their own floors and multipliers, plus *dated* money (longevity, holiday cash, uniform,
comp cash-outs) that lands in particular checks. Shift differentials are common but small ($0.35–$1.81/h
or 2–10%); the money is in overtime, events and lump sums.

## (b) Nurses and other hourly: what's different

| Rule | Who | Cite |
|---|---|---|

| OT on the 6th/7th consecutive day, or after 4 straight 12s | KAI, UC, HIL (police) | KAI 7th consecutive day 2× |
| **Rest-gap premium** (short turnaround paid at 1.5×) | UC 6 h, MN 7.5 h, UW 11 h, KAI 12 h, SCL 16 h | MN 4.5G |
| Late schedule-change premium | MN, WDOC, EBR (police) | WDOC 16.3.C |

| Differential by **clock hours only** | MN $2.75 6p–6a; SFM 8%; WDOC (day staff); SCL last 4 h of a 12 h day | MN 17.13 |
| No pyramiding / highest premium wins / total pay cap (4×) | UC, HEN, UW, SCL | SCL "No combinations of payments … shall exceed four (4) times" (16.2) |
| Standby/on-call as **non-worked** paid hours at a fraction | KAI ½, MN 15 min/h, WDOC 7%, UC $20/h | WDOC 32.20 |
| Report pay, missed-meal penalty hour, daily/weekly **guarantees** | UC, MN, KAI, SFM (8 h/day, 40 h/week) | SFM ¶200 |
| **Paycheck leveled to a fixed hours figure** regardless of hours worked | HEN: paid "one hundred twelve (112) regular hours per pay period, regardless of" | HEN 7.3 |
| 7(k) **182 h / 24 days** (fire) | HEN | 7.4 |
| Split-shift spread pay, platform/report time | SFM, AEMS | SFM ¶202–203 |
| **Monthly flat** differential | AEMS $100/mo | 6.6 |
| Holiday premium **split by clock** (1.65× before 8 a.m.) | HEN | 11.2 |
| Time rounding (6 min, 8 min → ¼ h, tenths) | HEN, UW, KAI, WDOC | UW 7.3 |

Nurses share police's need for multipliers, dated lump sums and window-designated differentials, and add
**daily and consecutive-day overtime** and **rest-gap premiums**. Fire adds **leveled pay** and a third
7(k) shape.

## (c) The pay model today, in primitives

What `index.html` can express right now, read from the code (line numbers as of `37025f5`):

| Primitive | What exists | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Base pay | One hourly `baseRate` per job; gross = paid hours × rate. No annual-salary input, no flat salary per period, no step table, no effective dates | `makeJob` `:1649`, `shiftGrossCents` `:1803` |
| Premium forms | Per shift type: `dollar` (+$/h), `percent` (math + sanitizer only — Settings can't create one), `multiplier`. Per-hour bonus table (charge / preceptor / on-call). Flat $ per shift (`custom`) | `hourlyRateCents` `:1793`, `BONUS` `:871`, `addDiff` `:6219` |
| Premium scope | **Whole shift, one type per shift.** No clock-window split, no shift-start rule, no stacking of two premiums on one shift beyond one differential + one bonus | `shiftGrossCents` |
| Overtime triggers | Per-shift flag at a hard-coded **1.5×** of the differential-inclusive rate; plus derived FLSA OT on a **40 h week** or **8/80**. No 7(k) period, no daily-only, no annual, no "flagged only", no tiered multiplier | `otMult` in `shiftGrossCents`; `overtimePremiumCents` `:1701`; `WORK_PERIODS` `:1629` |
| OT rate basis | `regular-rate` (FLSA blended) or `base-plus-diff` for the derived premium; flagged shifts always 1.5 × (base + diff) | `OT_METHODS` `:1634` |
| Minimum-guarantee events | None. A call-back or court minimum is logged by hand as max(actual, minimum) | — |
| Paid time off | PTO hours × base rate, no differentials, not counted toward OT | `periodPaycheck` `:1972` |
| Lump sums | None as such; a one-off custom flat bonus on a shift is the workaround | — |
| Pay period | Always 14 days. `payFrequency` is stored on the job and read by nothing | `periodStartOf` `:798`, `makeJob` |
| Deductions | Pre-tax and post-tax as flat $; FICA 7.65% or a custom %; federal and state flat %; custom withholdings % or flat $ | `computeNet` `:1931` |
| Multiple employers | Spine exists and is dormant: `jobs`, `groupHoursByJob`, per-job OT grouping | `groupHoursByJob` `:1752` |

**Probe, 2026-10-10** — one fortnight of each common police schedule priced by the app's own
`periodPaycheck` (`supabase/functions/_shared/badgebudget-core.mjs`), at an illustrative $45/h, with no
overtime actually worked. Any derived overtime is phantom:

| Schedule | Contracts using it | 40 h week | 8/80 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 h, 5-2 (80 h) | HIL, WAR, BOS | 0 | 0 |
| 4/10 (80 h) | SEA, POR, LBC, MIN | 0 | **+10.0%** |
| 3 on / 3 off, 10.5 h (84 h) | EBR | **+2.4%** | **+11.9%** |
| 12 h × 6 + 8 h Kelly day (80 h) | GRE | **+5.0%** | **+15.0%** |
| 12 h Pitman 2-2-3 (84 h) | CLE, FLO, MTL, CHE | **+4.8%** | **+16.7%** |

So the right setting depends on the schedule, and an officer on a 12-hour schedule has no correct choice.

**Since session 2 (2026-10-10)** these ten cells are pinned exactly in `tests/core.test.mjs`, and the
two settings added beside them — "only the shifts I mark as overtime" and N hours in a D-day period
(86 in 14 shown) — give **zero** on all five schedules.

## (d) What the model would need, ranked

Ranked by how many workers it unblocks × dollars at stake ÷ size. **Every row is wage-core (Invariant
3)** unless it says otherwise.

| # | Primitive | Who needs it (sample) | Error without it | Workaround today | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Configurable work period**: "only overtime I flag" plus N hours in D days (covers 40/7, 80/14, 86/14, 171/28, 182/24, 212/28, and 8/80's daily rule) | Police: 13 outside-tour, 6 on 12 h; fire | +2.4% to +16.7% of gross **every period** (probe) | Pick 40 or 8/80 by schedule; 12 h has none | Small. `WORK_PERIODS` becomes data `{days, hours, daily?}`; today's two values become two presets |
| 2 | **Per-shift multiplier + minimum hours** (call-back / court / report / show-up: max(actual, min) × mult) | Police 21/21 have minimums, 9/21 a multiplier > 1.5×; nurses 2× after 12 h; utility 2–3× | −33% to −67% on each event logged at actual hours; −40% on a 2.5× event at 1.5× | Type max(actual, min); scale hours by mult ÷ 1.5 | Small–medium. Replaces the hard-coded `otMult` |
| 3 | **Dated pay items**: lump sums and periodic pays (annual, semi-annual, monthly, one-off) | Police: holiday cash 8, longevity lump 5–7, uniform, comp cash-outs; nurses: ratification lumps ($1,000–$4,510) | The whole payment misses the check it lands in — often exactly the December or January money a February goal counts on | A custom flat bonus on a shift in that period | Medium. New list on the job, summed in `periodPaycheck` by date |
| 4 | **Salaried base**: flat pay per period plus a rate divisor separate from scheduled hours | Police: 11 salaried, 4 with a 2,080 divisor on longer schedules; fire leveled to 112 h | Per check ±5–10% when the rotation puts more or fewer hours in a period; OT rate 5% low if the divisor is fudged | Base = salary ÷ hours actually scheduled (right on average; OT rate low) | Medium–large. Every per-shift preview must stop pricing base hours |
| 5 | **Differential qualification**: by start / majority / clock-window hours, stored on the job | Police 7 / 2 / 3 (+2 named shift); nurses MN, UW, KAI; transit | Small for police ($0.35–$1.81/h); large for nurses ($10–$16.50/h defaults) | Split a shift in two by hand | Medium. **This is the blocked `BACKLOG.md` item** ("Differential *windows* on the job record"); this sample answers its open question — both conventions are common, so it must be a job field |
| 6 | **Separate-rate work excluded from OT hours** (paid details, second employer) | Police 10 with detail rules, 3 explicitly excluded; travel nurses | Detail hours feed phantom OT; wrong rate | A $ differential (still counts toward OT) | Medium. #1's "flagged only" removes most of the harm; the full fix is the dormant jobs spine |
| 7 | **Effective-dated rates** (steps, Jan 1 / July 1 / anniversary raises) | All | One step (~3–8%) × the share of the goal window after the date. Conservative direction | Edit the rate on the day | Medium |
| 8 | **Comp time**: OT banked, not paid now; cash-out later | Police 17; nurses UC, MN, UW | OT counted as cash this check when it isn't | Don't log banked OT; log the cash-out under #3 | Small. A per-shift "banked" flag |
| 9 | **Standby / on-call as non-worked paid hours** at a fraction or $/h | Police 6; nurses 4 | Missed entirely, or forced onto worked hours | Custom flat $ per day | Small–medium |
| 10 | **Pay frequency** other than biweekly | Police 3 of 16 stated; UW semi-monthly (inferred) | Every per-check figure and goal step has the wrong shape | None | **Large**: periods, goals, calendar chips |
| 11 | **Daily, consecutive-day and rest-gap overtime triggers** | Nurses (UC, KAI, UW, MN); HIL (6th/7th day) | Missed premium on those shifts | Flag the shift by hand | Medium, once #1 and #2 exist |
| 12 | **Stacking rules**: no pyramiding, highest-one-wins, caps, premiums on base only | UC, HEN, UW, SCL, SFM | An additive model overstates pay where pyramiding is barred | None | Medium. Last — second-order for every group here |
| — | **Medicare-only FICA** | PHL explicit; likely common in police and fire | ~6% of gross (~8% of net) if left at 7.65% | **Config exists** (FICA → Custom 1.45%) | **Not code, not wage-core math**: one onboarding question |
| — | **Percent differentials** | POR, BOS, CHE, UD; KAI | — | Enter 10% of base as $ | **UI only**: the math and sanitizer already support `percent`; Settings can't create one |

**Shape this as data, not a fork.** Rows 1–9 are each "one more field on the job, read by
`periodPaycheck`". That matches what `makeJob`'s comment already says lives on a job ("what an
EMPLOYER decides") and the `schedule_rules` shape in `schedule-rules-draft.md`. A profession then
becomes **a preset of field values** (8 h police, 4/10 police, 12 h police on 7(k), 24/48 fire,
12 h nurse on 8/80), not a code path. One schema serves nurses, police and the next hourly group; presets
are keyed to schedule archetypes, never to a single employer.

only; DCED's municipal statistics carry police spending, not terms; the Pennsylvania Municipal League's
wage survey is members-only. RTK route, if ever needed: the municipality's Open Records Officer; RTKL
§708(b)(8)(i) exempts negotiation material but "shall not apply to a final or executed contract"; five
business days to respond plus a 30-day extension; up to $0.25/page; appeal to the Office of Open Records
within 15 business days. Whether an Act 111 interest award's opinion is withholdable is **unverified**.

**Aggregators are not a shortcut.** Campaign Zero's site and PDF host are down (their coding covered
accountability, not pay); Ballotpedia's dashboard codes ~30 discipline and oversight questions with **no
pay questions** and many dead links; ACLU-CT's 91 Connecticut contracts all expired 2016–24. The only
national coding of police pay provisions found is **BLS Bulletin 1885 (1976)**, 504 contracts from
1972–73 (as reported by the research agent; not re-checked). For current pay *levels*, Ohio SERB's 2025
Wage Settlement Report gives police raises of **4.29% / 3.67% / 3.47%** in contract years 1–3 (206, 251,
239 contracts).

**Conclusion:** for building the model, no request is needed — Ohio + New Jersey + Iowa + city postings
give hundreds of current contracts, enough to confirm or reject any pattern above at scale. For any
individual user, the input is their stub, not their contract.

2. **How common is Medicare-only coverage among police?** Only Philadelphia states it in contract text;
   Ohio's OP&F members are generally outside Social Security (**unverified** from text). It decides

## (g) Field evidence, 2026-10-10: two real documents and three research passes

Gathered after sessions 1–2 shipped, to test the model against real pay rather than contract text.
**Real public pay stubs are scarce to an automated search:** the places workers post them (Reddit,
imgur, allnurses, union forums) blocked automated access, and every "police pay stub" page that did
load was a vendor template. So the evidence is two documents the owner supplied, plus official
payroll calendars, payroll tables, policies, statutes and court records. The three full reports
(examples tables, per-question tallies, every search tried) lived in the session scratchpad and are
gone when the container is reclaimed; the sources that carry each finding are linked here.

**The two documents the owner supplied.**
- **Philadelphia PD pay schedule, FY2026** (effective 2025-07-01, 3% raise, "261 Pay Days").
  Hourly = annual ÷ 261 days ÷ 8 = **annual ÷ 2,088**. The biweekly check is 10 × the daily rate.
  "Stress" pay is exactly **6% of base**, and longevity is paid **per day** (annual amount ÷ 261);
  both are inside the 1.5× overtime rate (e.g. step 5 OT 66.7823 + stress OT 4.0069 + longevity OT
  = 1.5 × the combined hourly). So a Philadelphia officer prices exactly today with one combined
  hourly rate, base × 1.06 + longevity ÷ 2,088, and FICA set to custom 1.45%.
- **A California police stub** (department redacted; period 2024-11-11 to 11-24, check 11-29).
  - **Medicare = 1.45% × (gross − health premium) exactly:** ($9,820.04 − $266.03) × 1.45% = $138.53.
    **No Social Security line.**
  - The night differential is paid at 1× on 161.5 hours, which includes the 85.5 overtime hours,
    beside a separate $274.67 "Overtime Special" line (unverified: plausibly a federal regular-rate
    top-up).
  - **Overtime rate $66.59 is 1.87× the listed $35.70 base, not 1.5×.** Unexplained, most likely a
    pay component not printed on the stub. Today's model cannot reproduce that line; session 3's
    per-shift multiplier could.

**Findings, by how much they change the plan.**

1. **The app overcharges FICA on pre-tax health premiums — a wage-core bug, verified three ways.**
   Section 125 health, dental and vision premiums are exempt from Social Security and Medicare;
   401(k), 403(b) salary-reduction and 457(b) deferrals are not ([IRS cafeteria-plan FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/faqs-for-government-entities-regarding-cafeteria-plans),
   [Pub 15](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15), [Pub 15-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15b)).
   The California stub shows it to the cent. A pension pick-up stays in the Medicare base
   ([IRS employer pick-ups](https://www.irs.gov/node/4221)): LaSalle IL's 2023 payroll table
   ([PDF](https://www.lasalle-il.gov/sites/default/files/forms-documents/2024-02/2023.pdf)) back-solves
   to a Medicare base equal to salary plus sick buyback for an officer with no health coverage (gap
   −$2.56) and a constant gap per health tier (≈$1,618 single, ≈$4,990 family) that does not scale with
   salary, as a 9.91% pension exclusion would. `computeNet` taxes FICA on full gross and has one
   "Pre-tax · 401k, health" field, so **a nurse who enters a $266 premium has take-home understated by
   ~$20 a check at 7.65%**. Filed in `BACKLOG.md` (dedicated, wage-core).
2. **Police and fire are often Medicare-only.** LaSalle and Bolingbrook IL
   ([2018 table](https://www.bolingbrook.gov/uploads/dm/11529/2018_Salaries__Benefits)): no Social
   Security on 115 of 115 officer rows, while civilians on the same sheets pay it. The firefighters'
   union estimates ~75% of its members are outside Social Security
   ([IAFF](https://www.iaff.org/wp-content/uploads/Events/LEGCON/2020/LegCon2019_documents_SocialSecurity.pdf));
   Fresno fire is Medicare-only. Plus Philadelphia and the California stub. This settles open question
   (f) 2 for the plan: **ask the Social Security question of police and fire** in session 5. Not
   checked: how many police *do* pay it (NYPD reportedly does).
3. **Which paycheck a 24–28-day period's overtime lands in is not one rule.** The federal default is
   the check for the pay period in which the work period ends ([29 CFR 778.106](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/778.106)),
   which is what session 2 built, and what aligned calendars do (Guadalupe County TX
   [payroll dates](https://www.guadalupetx.gov/page/open/1331/0/Payroll_Dates.pdf); Holly Springs NC,
   partial). But **Cambria CSD (CA) pays a 24-day cycle one check later in 27 of 31 cycles**
   ([2024](https://cambriacsd.specialdistrict.org/files/5b19ff6de/2024+Payroll+Calendar+Final.pdf),
   [2025](https://cambriacsd.specialdistrict.org/files/67cd8299d/2025+Payroll+Calendar+FINAL.pdf)
   calendars), a California grand jury found policies allowing "no later than one pay period after"
   ([Tuolumne](https://www.tuolumne.courts.ca.gov/system/files/general/2025-2026-tuolumne-county-civil-grand-jury-twain-harte-fire-district-payroll-practices-report.pdf)),
   and a Texas city pays per-period checks plus a settlement check
   ([DOL FLSA2018-5](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/WHD/legacy/files/2018_01_05_05_FLSA.pdf)).
   One verified misaligned calendar, so how common "+1 check" is stays **unverified**. Filed: a
   "paid on this check / the next check" setting, before the Fire & EMS persona.
4. **The salary-to-hourly divisor varies far more than 2,080.** Police: 2,080 (San Diego, Phoenix,
   Boston, NY State, Somerville), 2,088 (Philadelphia, South Dakota), 2,087 (federal), 1,950 (Agawam MA,
   for overtime). Fire: 2,080 to 2,994 (2,756 federal, 2,912 and 2,920 common) — **up to 44% apart**.
   Onboarding's ÷2,080 is fine for Healthcare and Other hourly; Law enforcement and Fire & EMS need the
   divisor as an input. Filed against session 5.
5. **Fire pay is often leveled.** Same regular hours every check whatever the shifts fall on: 9
   verified (e.g. Waco, Springfield OR [CBA](https://springfield-or.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2021-2024-IAFF-FF-CBA.pdf),
   federal 106 h a check [OPM/DoD](https://dcpas.osd.mil/sites/default/files/2021-04/FederalFirefighterPay.pdf),
   Coconut Creek 96 h), against 11 of 13 Tennessee cities paying actual hours
   ([MTAS survey](https://mtas.tennessee.edu/system/files/mrln/mknowledge/main/MTAS%20Firefighter%20Scheduling%20Survey%20Results%20July%202024.pdf)).
   Confirms primitive 4 (salaried / leveled base) is needed before fire is priced per check.
6. **Overtime is 1.5× everywhere it was stated** (8 police sources, 14 fire). Whether differentials
   and incentives sit inside the overtime rate varies, and three police departments (Agawam, Oakland,
   Portland) left them out until a court, lawsuit or grievance put them in. No source paid a
   differential as its own 1.5× line. Hospital practice varies too: UC pays straight time plus a
   separate premium line ([earn codes](https://bfs.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2020-08/Earn_Codes_List_Expanded.pdf)),
   a Washington contract puts the differential inside the overtime rate and pays overtime only past
   12 h a day or 40 a week ([MultiCare](https://cdn.wsna.org/assets/entry-assets/906542/MBNIC-contract-APPENDIX-B.pdf)).
   Both support keeping the work period and OT method as settings rather than one rule.
7. **Pay lag** from period end to check: 5–7 days (police, fire, the California stub), 11 days at UC
   ([Berkeley dates](https://controller.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/2025_biweekly_pay_dates.pdf)).
   Nothing in the model depends on it today.
